HARVEST SOS is a wearable energy-harvesting band designed for disaster survivors and rescue workers in environments where cell networks have failed and external charging is unavailable. The wristband simultaneously draws from four energy sources — electromagnetic kinetic (wrist motion), thermoelectric (skin-to-air temperature difference), photovoltaic (flexible solar cells), and piezoelectric (strap flexion) — and the core engineering question driving the project is whether an adaptive power-management system, which continuously polls each source and routes load to the strongest active combination, can meaningfully extend the wearable's operating time compared to using a single source or passively combining all four. That comparison — single source vs. passive sum vs. adaptive HARVEST — is the central experiment, and the result is left open: the prototype data will determine whether the gain is 10%, 25%, or something else entirely. The harvested and stored energy powers a LoRa-based SOS module that operates entirely independently of cellular infrastructure: the wearable broadcasts GPS coordinates (when a satellite fix is available) or a device-ID-only distress signal (when GPS lock is lost, such as under rubble) on 865 MHz, and neighbouring HARVEST units can relay the packet onward, extending reach without any towers. A lightweight rescue-side dashboard receives these packets and displays SOS status, device ID, last known location, and estimated battery level — but the wearable never depends on the dashboard to function; the SOS broadcasts continuously regardless. Two important honesty corrections run through the whole proposal: all performance figures are clearly marked as estimated or target values pending experimental verification, and the product is framed as eliminating cellular-network dependency and reducing external charging dependence — not as being "battery-free" (it has a LiPo) or "satellite-free" (GPS is satellite-based).
